## 1. Why it exists

Patients, employees and candidates have rights over their data. The most common in a clinic are **access** (a copy of the record), **erasure** ("delete my data") and **objection** ("stop sending me messages"). The law sets the clock and the minimum content of the answer, but not the way the clinic organises itself. That is what the procedure is for.

Four facts make this harder in a small practice than it looks:

- **A request can be made in any form.** Said at the reception desk, in an e-mail, in a phone call. It does not have to quote the GDPR or use a form. The first person who hears it is usually not the person who can answer it.
- **The clock starts on receipt, not on logging.** The reply is due **within one month** (Art. 12(3)), with at most two further months for complex cases, and the person must be told of the extension in the first month.
- **Medical records collide with erasure.** Most of what a clinic holds, it must keep by law. The right answer to "delete everything" is usually a **partial refusal**, well explained.
- **The reply itself can be a breach.** Sending a patient's record to the wrong person, or over an insecure channel, is the most likely way a rights request turns into an incident.

### Deadlines

| Stage | Deadline |
|---|---|
| Passing the request to the handler | At most 1 working day from receipt. |
| Logging and acknowledgement (T1) | At most 3 working days. |
| Reply on the substance | Without undue delay and within one month of receiving the request (Art. 12(3) GDPR). Recommended internal target: 25 calendar days. |
| Extension for complex or numerous requests | By up to two further months. The person is told of the extension and the reason within the first month (T11). |
| Identity check / clarifications | Requested without delay (T2). The one-month period runs from receipt of the information needed. Interpretation per EDPB guidance on the right of access – to be confirmed. |
| Refusal to act on a request | The person is told without delay and within one month of the reasons, and of the right to complain and to go to court (Art. 12(4) GDPR) (T12). |

Exercising rights is **free of charge**. A reasonable fee based on administrative costs may be charged only for further copies (Art. 15(3) GDPR), or the request may be refused if it is manifestly unfounded or excessive (Art. 12(5) GDPR). The clinic must be able to show why a request is manifestly unfounded or excessive.

## 2. How the procedure works

### Roles

| Role | Responsibilities |
|---|---|
| All staff | Recognise a request (for example: "I want a copy of my file", "delete my data", "I don't want any more messages") and pass it to the request handler within one working day. They do not answer on the substance themselves. |
| Request handler (Dr. A, owner-physician) | Logs the request, calculates the deadlines, verifies identity, coordinates the search for data, drafts the reply and keeps the register. |
| Physician in charge (Dr. A) | Decides on requests concerning medical data (access, rectification, erasure, restriction), assesses the exceptions and the effect on care, and approves the reply. |
| Providers (Practice Software Provider, Accounting Firm, Messaging Provider) | Carry out technical operations at the clinic's request (export, deletion, restriction) and confirm in writing within the time the clinic sets. |

### Nine steps

1. Receive and pass on. Any employee who receives a request notes it (who, what, when, which channel) and passes it to the handler.
2. Log and set the deadline. The handler enters it in the request register (section 8) and calculates the deadline: date received + one month.
3. Acknowledge. Send template T1.
4. Verify identity and clarify the request. Apply the rules in section 5. If it is unclear what data or which right is meant, ask (T2).
5. Identify the right and search for the data. Decide which right is being exercised (section 6) and check every place the data may be: the medical application, the paper archive, the consultation register, e-mail and phone, appointments and messages (the messaging provider), invoices and accounting, results received from laboratories, marketing lists, website form submissions. Video images are searched only if the person gives the date, time and area.
6. Assess. Analyse the exceptions and limits (legal retention duties, professional secrecy, other people's rights). For medical data the physician in charge decides. Complete the internal checklist (section 7).
7. Carry out and notify recipients. Carry out the operation (copy, correction, deletion, restriction). For rectification, erasure and restriction, tell each recipient who received the data, unless this is impossible or disproportionate (Art. 19 GDPR) (T13).
8. Reply. Send the written reply using the right template (section 9 and the template library), through a secure channel (see section 5). Any reply that refuses, wholly or partly, gives the reason and the right to complain.
9. Close. Update the register, keep the request file under RET-09 (3 years) and note any lesson learned. If the request revealed a systemic problem (for example, data kept past its period), update the ROPA and the retention schedule. If it revealed a data breach, apply the incident procedure.

### Verifying identity and sending the reply

- Principle: medical data is not given to a person whose identity has not been verified. Verification is proportionate to the risk: stricter for medical data than for an unsubscribe request.
- At the clinic: check the ID document without copying it. The ID is checked and not copied.
- By e-mail: reply only to the address already in the file. If the request comes from another address, confirm identity by additional means (for example, a call to the number on file or a visit to the clinic). Avoid asking for copies of ID unless strictly necessary.
- By phone: no medical data is given; the person is invited to submit the request in writing or at the clinic.
- Representatives: check the representative's identity and proof of authority (parent or guardian for minors; a signed authorisation from the patient for authorised persons).
- Sending the reply: medical data is handed over in person or sent through a secure channel (a password-protected file by e-mail, with the password given by phone, or collection in person). Personal messaging apps are not used.

## 3. Worked case: a patient asks for her file and for deletion

**Case REQ-2027-001** (all details fictional). On Monday 1 March 2027 a patient, referred to in the register as P-01, tells the reception desk: *"I'd like a copy of my file, and please delete everything and stop sending me messages."*

| When | What happens | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Mon 1 Mar (day 0) | Receptionist D recognises **two** requests (access, and erasure with objection to messages) and passes them to Dr. A the same day. | Staff do not answer on the substance. The one-working-day rule is met. |
| Tue 2 Mar (day 1) | Dr. A logs it as REQ-2027-001. Deadline: 1 April. Internal target: 26 March. **T1** is sent. | Acknowledgement within 3 working days. |
| Thu 4 Mar | The patient comes in, shows her ID, which is checked and not copied. The date and method go in the register. | Identity is verified before medical data is released. |
| 4 to 8 Mar | Search of all the places the data may be: practice software, paper archive, consultation register, e-mail (laboratory results), messaging provider, invoices (accounting firm). | Step 5 of the procedure. A search of only one system is the usual mistake. |
| Mon 8 Mar | The promotional contact is deleted. The messaging provider is told (**T13**) and confirms deletion the next day. | Art. 19: recipients are told of an erasure. Marketing has no retention duty, so this part is granted at once. |
| 8 to 12 Mar | Dr. A reviews the copy. A companion's name in one note is removed from the copy. Erasure of the medical record, test results and invoices is refused, because the law requires them to be kept. | Art. 15(4): other people's data is protected. Art. 17(3)(b): legal retention. |
| Mon 15 Mar (day 14) | **T3** (access) and **T6** (partial refusal) are handed over. The copy is a password-protected file; the password is given by phone. | Reply on the substance well inside the 25-day target. The refusal states the reason and the right to complain. |
| 15 Mar | Register updated. The file is kept for 3 years (RET-09). | No medical data goes in the register. |

**What the case shows**

- One conversation held *two* requests, and each needed its own answer.
- The patient lost nothing she could lawfully lose: her marketing contact was deleted at once, and she was told exactly why the rest stays and for how long.
- Every step is evidenced in the register, so the clinic can show an inspector or the patient what it did and when.

## 4. Special situations

- Minors: rights are exercised through parents or legal representatives. The physician assesses case by case where a minor has enough understanding. Dr. A decides case by case.
- Deceased persons: the GDPR does not apply to the data of deceased persons, but professional secrecy continues. Requests from relatives are assessed case by case with the physician in charge, under national law. To be confirmed.
- Requests from authorities, lawyers, insurers or employers: these are not rights requests. Check the legal basis or the patient's written agreement and decide with the physician in charge before any disclosure.
- Requests about someone else's data (for example, a relative asking for an adult's file): no data is given without the person's written agreement or a legal basis.
- CCTV access: ask for the date, time slot and area. If the images were deleted at the end of the period, tell the person. If they exist, offer viewing under supervision or a copy that protects other people's identity (T14). Images are not given to third parties; police requests are handled only on a written request with a legal basis.
- Repeated or excessive requests: assessed by the physician and the handler; a refusal or fee must be reasoned and documented (T12).
- Dispute or inspection under way: apply the legal hold; data is not deleted and the person is told why.
- Visiting physician's patients: records kept by the visiting physician for his own patients are his responsibility as a separate controller. The request is passed to him the same day and logged here. The arrangement is to be confirmed.

### Internal checklist for every request

- [ ] The request is logged (date received, channel) and the deadline is calculated.
- [ ] The acknowledgement (T1) has been sent.
- [ ] The person's identity (or the representative's authority) is verified; date and method are noted.
- [ ] The right being exercised is identified and the request is clear.
- [ ] All places where the data may be have been checked.
- [ ] Exceptions and limits have been assessed (retention duties, professional secrecy, third-party rights, dispute).
- [ ] The physician in charge approved the reply (for medical data).
- [ ] The operation was carried out (copy, correction, deletion, restriction), including by providers.
- [ ] Recipients were informed (Art. 19), if applicable.
- [ ] The reply was sent through a secure channel and, for a refusal, gives the reason and the right to complain.
- [ ] The register is updated and the file is kept under RET-09.

## 6. Design decisions

**1. Everyone must recognise a request; almost no one answers it.** The most frequent failure is a request lost at the front desk. The procedure asks all staff to do one small thing, pass it on within one working day, and leaves the substance to two people. This links to the staff training (module 8).

**2. Two clocks inside the legal one.** An acknowledgement within 3 working days, and an internal reply target of 25 days, leave a margin inside the one-month legal limit for a missing document or an absent physician.

**3. Partial refusal as the default pattern for erasure.** A clinic rarely has to say a flat "no" or a flat "yes". Template T6 is built for the real answer: *this part is deleted now, this part is kept because of the law, until this date.*

**4. A physician decides on medical data.** Not the receptionist, not the privacy adviser. The medical consequences of a restriction or correction are a clinical judgement, and the procedure names who covers when that person is away.

**5. Identity checks scaled to risk.** Medical data is released only after identity is verified. An unsubscribe request needs almost none. IDs are checked, not copied, and no medical data goes by phone or messaging app.

**6. The register holds no medical data.** It records who, when, what right, what decision and when, using references. A register of health information would itself be a risk.

**7. Templates for the hard cases too.** Besides the standard replies, the library covers extensions (T11), refusals of manifestly unfounded or excessive requests (T12), notices to recipients (T13) and CCTV access (T14), the cases staff are most likely to get wrong when improvising.

**8. Test it once.** Before the first real request, run a simulated access request through the whole procedure. It is the cheapest way to find the missing system in the search list.

**9. Records held by the visiting physician.** Requests about his own patients' records are passed to him the same day and logged here. The arrangement is flagged as *to be confirmed*, matching the ROPA.

## 7. Romanian national layer

> **Romania-specific points** (the procedure applies anywhere in the EU)
>
> - **Authority for complaints.** ANSPDCP (www.dataprotection.ro, anspdcp@dataprotection.ro). Every refusal template names it.
> - **Patient rights law.** Law no. 46/2003 sets rules on patient information and access to medical documents. *To be confirmed: the specific articles that apply to copies of the record.*
> - **National ID number (CNP).** Never needed to verify identity when an ID card can be checked in person; any copy follows the safeguards in Art. 4 of Law no. 190/2018.
> - **Deceased patients and minors.** The GDPR does not cover deceased persons, but secrecy continues; requests from relatives and from or about minors are decided case by case under national law. *To be confirmed with a lawyer.*
> - **Period guidance on access.** How the one-month clock behaves when the clinic asks for more identity information is addressed in EDPB guidance on the right of access. *To be confirmed.*

## 8. Blank templates

The blank procedure and the template library follow the structure above. Yellow fields are for you to complete.

**How to use them**

1. Name the request handler and the physician in charge, and a deputy for each.
2. List every place your clinic holds data, so the search step is complete. Start from your ROPA.
3. Decide the secure channel for sending medical data, and write it down in section 5.
4. Put the letterhead and contact details into the templates once.
5. Train staff to recognise and pass on a request (15 minutes is enough).
6. Run one simulated access request before the first real one.
