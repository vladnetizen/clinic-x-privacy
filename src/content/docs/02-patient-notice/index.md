## 1. Why it exists

A privacy notice answers one question for the patient: *what happens to my data?* It is usually the first privacy document a patient, an inspector or a regulator sees, and it is the one most small clinics already have.

The law asks for three things of it:

- **Give it at the right time.** When the data is collected (Art. 13), or within a reasonable time if the data came from someone else (Art. 14).
- **Make it understandable.** "Concise, transparent, intelligible and easily accessible form, using clear and plain language" (Art. 12(1)).
- **Include the required content.** The table below lists it, and shows where each item sits in the Clinic X notice.

| What the GDPR requires | Source | Where in notice v2.0 |
|---|---|---|
| Identity and contact details of the controller | Art. 13(1)(a) | Header, section 8 |
| Contact details of the DPO, if there is one | Art. 13(1)(b) | Section 8 |
| Purposes and legal basis | Art. 13(1)(c) | Sections 2 and 3 |
| Legitimate interests relied on | Art. 13(1)(d) | Sections 3 and 7 |
| Recipients or categories of recipients | Art. 13(1)(e) | Section 5 |
| Transfers outside the EU/EEA | Art. 13(1)(f) | Section 5 |
| Retention period, or the criteria used | Art. 13(2)(a) | Section 4 |
| Rights: access, rectification, erasure, restriction, objection, portability | Art. 13(2)(b) | Section 6 |
| Right to withdraw consent (if consent is used) | Art. 13(2)(c) | Section 6 |
| Right to complain to the supervisory authority | Art. 13(2)(d) | Section 6 |
| Whether providing data is a legal or contractual requirement, and the consequences of not providing it | Art. 13(2)(e) | Section 3 |
| Automated decisions and profiling | Art. 13(2)(f) | Section 5 |
| Where the data came from, if not the patient | Art. 14(2)(f) | Section 1 |

**What a notice is not.** It is not a consent form, not a record of processing (that is the ROPA, module 3) and not a retention schedule (module 4). Keeping those separate is what keeps the notice short and stable.

### Mistakes common in small practices

- A generic template that does not match what the clinic actually does.
- Treating consent as the basis for medical care. Care rests on contract, legal obligation and the health-care condition in Art. 9(2)(h). Consent to a medical procedure is a different thing from GDPR consent to processing.
- A signature line that looks like consent but is only a confirmation of reading.
- A table of every retention period inside the notice. It bloats the document and goes stale.
- The clinic is called by two different names in the same notice.
- Cameras mentioned with no area, no retention period and no sign at the door.
- Promises the clinic cannot keep, such as "we never share your data".

### What v1.0 got right

- **Correct legal bases for the core activities.** Care rests on Art. 6(1)(b) and (c) with Art. 9(2)(h). It does not ask patients to "consent" to treatment.
- **CCTV treated properly.** Its own section, legitimate interest as the basis, common areas only, a stated storage period and restricted access.
- **Erasure framed realistically.** The right is described with its limit for records the clinic must keep.
- **Plain language.** Short sentences, no dense legal paragraphs.

### Reviewer's notes on v1.0

When v1.0 was first reviewed, notes were left for the clinic directly in the margin of the document. In summary:

- **Cameras only in common areas.** Never in consultation rooms, toilets or other private spaces. Only authorised staff may see the footage.
- **Do not keep recordings permanently.** There is no single fixed period for every case, but they should be deleted after a few days.
- **Retention periods by category** were suggested to the clinic, but flagged as dependent on their actual storage and on the law in force.
- **Erasure has limits in a clinic.** Some data can be deleted (non-essential contact data, online accounts, marketing data); records the clinic must keep cannot.
- **The signature line is only an acknowledgement.** It is not consent and may be removed.

Margin notes do not belong in a document handed to patients. In v2.0 they moved to a separate internal guide for the clinic, and the detail they pointed at became the retention schedule (module 4).

### What was missing

v1.0 was a solid notice, but a notice answers only "what do we tell patients?". Compared with the Art. 13 checklist above it lacked the points in the change log below: the source of the data, the processors, transfers and automated decisions, how to exercise rights and in what time, a contact for data protection questions, and the retention criteria.

## 4. Design decisions

**1. Retention by criteria, not by category and year.** The notice says which rules set the period (health law, accounting law) and that exact periods are available on request. The detailed schedule stays internal (module 4). A table of periods in a patient notice adds length, goes stale when the law or the clinic's practice changes, and is not required by Art. 13(2)(a).

**2. The CCTV period stays in the notice.** Unlike the records, the camera period is a simple fact that is the same for everyone and is the detail people ask about. It appears in section 7 and on the sign at the door, consistent with the layered approach of EDPB Guidelines 3/2019.

**3. The acknowledgement is optional and is not consent.** Asking a patient to sign "I have read this" is harmless if it is labelled for what it is. The risk is a signature line that patients and staff treat as consent for everything. v2.0 says so in one sentence, and the guide for the clinic notes it can be removed.

**4. Consent only where it is real.** Medical care rests on contract, legal obligation and Art. 9(2)(h). Consent appears only for promotional communications, where the patient can say no without losing any service.

**5. Optional passages are marked "delete if not applicable".** A small clinic may not send marketing messages or use an outside e-mail service. Instead of a notice that lists everything a larger practice might do, each optional passage is flagged for deletion, so the final notice says only what is true.

**6. The legitimate-interest basis for CCTV is backed by a written assessment.** The notice states the basis; the reasoning behind it is in module 10 (LIA).

## 5. Romanian national layer

> **Romania-specific points** (the EU core of this page applies anywhere in the EU)
>
> - **National ID number (CNP).** Processed with the safeguards in Art. 4 of Law no. 190/2018.
> - **Supervisory authority.** ANSPDCP (www.dataprotection.ro). The notice names it so patients know where to complain.
> - **Video surveillance.** ANSPDCP Decision no. 52/2012 is the usual reference for storage limits, with a maximum of 30 days. *To be confirmed: final published text.* Staff monitoring is governed by Art. 5 of Law no. 190/2018, which also limits storage to 30 days and requires prior consultation of employee representatives (module 11).
> - **Patient rights and medical secrecy.** The patient rights law (Law no. 46/2003) is relevant to confidentiality and records. *To be confirmed: the specific articles.*
> - **Medical record retention periods.** See module 4. *To be confirmed* before relying on them.

## 6. Putting it into use

A notice only works if patients can actually find it.

- [ ] Offer it at the first visit and keep a printed copy at reception.
- [ ] Put the camera sign (Annex I) at the entrance.
- [ ] Publish it on the clinic's website if there is one.
- [ ] Make sure reception staff know where it is and can say what it covers.
- [ ] Inform employees separately about cameras and their own data (module 11).
- [ ] Sign processing agreements with the providers named in section 5 (module 9).
- [ ] Record the version number and date; keep earlier versions.
- [ ] Review once a year, and every time a new system, purpose or camera is added.
