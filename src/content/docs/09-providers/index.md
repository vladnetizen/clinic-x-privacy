## 1. Why it exists

A clinic does not hold its data alone. The practice software, the e-mail, the text messages, the accountant and the CCTV installer all see some of it. If one of them fails, the patient will hold the clinic responsible.

The GDPR puts three duties on the clinic:

- **Know the role of each provider.** A *processor* acts on the clinic's instructions and needs an Art. 28 contract. A *separate controller* decides for itself and does not.
- **Bind processors in writing.** Art. 28(3) lists what the contract must say. Without it the clinic breaches the GDPR even if the provider is excellent.
- **Choose providers with care.** Art. 28(1) asks for processors that give "sufficient guarantees". In practice that means checking, before signing and then yearly.

Three things go wrong in small practices: no contract at all, a contract nobody read, and providers nobody remembers until something happens.

## 2. Who is a processor

| Provider | Usual role | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Practice software, IT support and hosting | Processor | Art. 28 contract required. Access to health data calls for extra care. |
| Accountant, accounting and payroll firm | Processor for payroll; may be a separate controller for its own legal duties | Clarified in the ROPA: processor for payroll, independent for statutory accounting duties. |
| SMS and e-mail providers | Processor | Art. 28 contract; check where the data is stored. |
| CCTV maintenance, paper shredding, website hosting | Processor if they can reach the data | A CCTV installer who cannot see recordings may not be a processor; document the reason. |
| Laboratories, partner clinics, referred specialists | Separate controller (usually) | No Art. 28 contract; named as recipients in the patient notice. A short data-sharing note is good practice. |
| Occupational health, card payments, tax and health-insurance authorities | Separate controller or authority | No Art. 28 contract. |

If in doubt, treat the provider as a processor and ask for the contract. It is the safer error.

### Six steps

1. List the providers from the ROPA and the asset inventory, and decide the role of each.
2. Review the provider with the evaluation checklist before the contract, and again every year.
3. Sign the agreement. Use the template. Large providers offer their own terms: check them against the Art. 28(3) list and ask for an addendum if something is missing.
4. Enter the provider in the register and in the ROPA.
5. Review every year: contract, sub-processors, data location, incidents.
6. At the end of the relationship, ask for the data to be returned or deleted, and for written confirmation.

## 4. Worked review: the Practice Software Provider (V-01)

The evaluation checklist has six areas: general, location and transfers, security, staff and sub-processors, incidents and rights, and the end of the relationship. The provider answers in writing. The clinic then marks each answer OK, To clarify or No.

Dr. A did the review on **Tuesday 12 January 2027**. The answers are fictional.

| Area | Question | Answer | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| General | What service does it provide, and which of the practice's data does it handle? | Practice management and electronic records: patient files, appointments, billing, attached images. Includes health data and the national ID number. | ✔ OK |
| General | Is it a processor or a separate controller? | Processor. It acts on the practice's instructions and has no purposes of its own. | ✔ OK |
| Location and transfers | Where are the data stored and processed? In the EU/EEA? | In an EU data centre (provider's statement). | ✔ OK |
| Location and transfers | Can anyone reach the data from outside the EEA (for example support staff)? On what basis? | Support is staffed in the EU. An engineering team outside the EEA may get temporary, logged access to fix a fault, relying on standard contractual clauses. | ◐ To clarify |
| Security | Individual accounts and two-step verification? | Individual accounts. Two-step verification is available and the practice's administrator can make it mandatory. | ✔ OK |
| Security | Data encrypted in transit and at rest? | Yes: encrypted connections, and encrypted storage (provider's statement). | ✔ OK |
| Security | Backups: how often, where, tested restores? | Daily, encrypted, in the EU, kept 90 days. The provider tests a restore once a year and can restore a single record on request. | ✔ OK |
| Security | Access logs available? | Yes, per-user access logs in the application; export on request within 5 working days. | ✔ OK |
| Security | Updates and vulnerability handling? | Monthly releases; critical fixes within days (provider's statement). | ✔ OK |
| Security | Certifications or independent reports (for example ISO 27001)? Not required, but a useful sign. | States that it holds ISO/IEC 27001. A copy of the certificate has been requested. | ◐ To clarify |
| Staff and sub-processors | Staff bound by confidentiality and trained? | Yes, in employment contracts, with yearly training (provider's statement). | ✔ OK |
| Staff and sub-processors | Sub-processors? Which, where, and how are changes notified? | Two, listed in Annex 3 of the agreement (cloud infrastructure and backup storage), both in the EU. 30 days' notice of changes. | ✔ OK |
| Incidents and rights | How fast does it report an incident? Is there a written procedure? | Within 24 hours, by contract (Art. 10). Written procedure. | ✔ OK |
| Incidents and rights | Incidents in the last 2 years? What was done? | None affecting clients' data (provider's statement). | ✔ OK |
| Incidents and rights | How does it help with patients' requests (export, deletion, correction)? | Export of a patient's record as PDF or CSV within 5 working days; deletion on instruction. | ✔ OK |
| End of the relationship | How are data returned or deleted at the end? Within what time? Written confirmation? | Export in a structured format, deletion within 30 days, backups roll off within 90 days, written confirmation. | ✔ OK |

**Conclusion (V-01, review of 12 January 2027, by Dr. A): acceptable with conditions.** (1) Make two-step verification mandatory, by 31 January 2027. (2) Obtain the ISO/IEC 27001 certificate, by 31 January 2027. (3) Get written confirmation that no access from outside the EEA happens without the practice's prior written approval, by 31 January 2027. (4) Test an export of one patient record, by 28 February 2027.

**What the review shows**

- **Most answers were fine; the useful ones were the exceptions.** The two "to clarify" items were access from outside the EEA and the missing certificate. Both could have stayed invisible without a written question.
- **The review closes a gap in the security register (module 7).** SEC-03 said two-step verification was "to be confirmed with the provider". It exists. What remains is switching it on, which is condition 1.
- **The ROPA keeps saying "no transfers" until the provider answers.** If it confirms occasional access from outside the EEA, the ROPA and the patient notice change.

## 5. Checking a large provider's own terms: the E-mail Provider (V-06)

Large providers will not sign the clinic's template. They offer their own terms. The check is a table: for each Art. 28(3) element, where is it, and is it there?

| Required element (Art. 28(3)) | Where in the provider's agreement | Found |
|---|---|---|
| Subject, duration, nature and purpose; types of data; categories of people | Section 1 and Annex 1 | ✔ Yes |
| Processing only on the controller's documented instructions (including for transfers); duty to warn if an instruction breaks the law | Section 2 | ✔ Yes |
| Confidentiality of the people authorised to process | Section 3 | ✔ Yes |
| Security measures (Art. 32) | Section 4 and Annex 2 | ✔ Yes |
| Conditions for sub-processors (authorisation, same obligations, liability) | Section 5, public list | ✔ Yes |
| Help with data subjects' requests | Section 6 | ✔ Yes |
| Help with Art. 32 to 36 (security, breaches, impact assessments) | Section 7 | ✔ Yes |
| Deletion or return of data at the end | Section 8: deletion within 180 days of the end | ◐ Partly |
| Information and audits | Section 9: audit reports and certifications, on-site audits only in limited cases | ◐ Partly |
| Clear, short deadline to report a breach | Section 10: “without undue delay”, no fixed hours | ◐ Partly |
| Location of data and transfer mechanism (Chapter V) | Section 11: EU region; transfers under adequacy decisions or standard clauses | ✔ Yes |

**Conclusion (V-06, standard terms of the E-mail Provider, checked on 12 January 2027): acceptable.** The three “partly” items are recorded in the register as accepted deviations: deletion in 180 days, audit by reports, no fixed breach deadline. A small practice cannot renegotiate a large provider's terms, so it documents the choice and relies on the provider's published commitments. A review of support access from outside the EEA is planned for 9 February 2027.

## 7. What was verified, and what was not

**Checked against sources**

- **Standard clauses.** The European Commission has published standard contractual clauses between controllers and processors under Art. 28(7) ([Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/915](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dec_impl/2021/915/oj) of 4 June 2021). They are a ready-made alternative to the template.
- **Controller and processor roles.** The role of a provider depends on the facts, as the EDPB sets out in its [Guidelines 07/2020](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/documents/2023-10/EDPB_guidelines_202007_controllerprocessor_final_en.pdf) (final version, adopted 7 July 2021). The labels in this case study are the author's reading, and the guidelines are where to go for a doubtful case.

**Not verified, to be confirmed**

- **The accountant's role.** The ROPA treats the accounting firm as a processor for payroll and as independent for statutory accounting duties. That split is a reasoned position, not a settled rule.
- **The CCTV installer.** It is a processor only if it can reach recordings. The clinic's position is that it cannot, and that has to be checked in the review.
- **The visiting physician.** His independent-controller status and the arrangement for using the clinic's system are flagged *to be confirmed* in the ROPA.
- **Time limits in the agreement.** 24 hours, 30 days, 5 working days and 15 days are recommendations, not legal figures.
- **A legal review** of the agreement is recommended before signing a real one.

## 8. Design decisions

**1. A register that includes the providers who do not need a contract.** Four of the twelve are separate controllers. Listing them, with the reason, shows the clinic thought about each role and did not skip the ones that are inconvenient.

**2. One example done in full, one done as a check.** V-01 shows the full review. V-06 shows the other everyday situation: a large provider with its own terms, where the job is to find the gaps and decide.

**3. Deviations are recorded, not hidden.** Three gaps in the E-mail Provider's terms are accepted and written down with the reason. That is more honest, and more defensible, than a tick in every box.

**4. Review in order of risk.** The practice software provider, who holds the records, goes first. The shredding provider goes last. Dates are in the register, so the next owner can see what is due.

**5. Short, specific time limits.** 24 hours to report a breach matches the clinic's own 72-hour clock (module 6): the clinic needs time to assess before it notifies.

**6. The agreement protects the exit.** Return or deletion at the end, with a date and a written confirmation, matters as much as the start. It is usually forgotten until the clinic changes software.

**7. Two documents, not one.** The template stays blank for reuse. The completed example shows what a filled-in agreement and its annexes look like.

## 9. Romanian national layer

> **Romania-specific points** (the structure applies anywhere in the EU)
>
> - **Governing law.** The agreement is governed by Romanian law and names a competent Romanian court. National provisions that supplement the GDPR include Law no. 190/2018.
> - **Health authorities.** The county health insurance fund and other authorities receive data on a legal basis and are separate controllers. They need no Art. 28 contract, but are named as recipients in the patient notice.
> - **Medical secrecy.** The clauses on confidentiality treat health data as covered by professional secrecy. *To be confirmed: the sources.*
> - **Language of the contract.** The agreement can be in Romanian or English; for a Romanian clinic, a Romanian version is advisable for use before authorities.

## 10. Blank templates

The blank pack and the blank agreement follow the structure above. Yellow fields are for you to complete.

**How to use them**

1. List every provider from your ROPA and asset inventory, and decide the role of each.
2. Start with the practice software provider, the accountant, the messaging or e-mail provider, and the CCTV maintenance firm.
3. For each processor, either sign the agreement or check the provider's own terms with the Art. 28(3) checklist.
4. Review each processor once, then every year, and write the date in the register.
5. Record deviations you accept, and why.
6. Update the ROPA and the patient notice when a provider's role or location changes.
